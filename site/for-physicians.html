For referring physicians

Referring a patient

Dr. Verma welcomes referrals usually from ophthalmologists, optometrists, neurologists, neurosurgeons, pediatricians, endocrinologists, rheumatologists, primary care physicians, and emergency departments, but anyone can refer a patient for neuro-ophthalmic consultation. A consultation note goes back to the referring physician after every new visit.

Referrals welcome for our waitlist

The office is opening soon and is not yet seeing patients. You can refer patients now: add them to our waitlist with the referral form, and we will contact each patient when scheduling opens. Patients who need urgent care should continue with their current doctors or go to the emergency room.

Refer a patient

How to refer

  1. Complete the short referral form (neuroophtho.com/refer) with the patient's name and phone number and your office's contact details.
  2. Please don't send medical records through the form. We will ask for the reason for referral, recent eye exam notes, visual fields or OCT, and any MRI or CT reports when we schedule.
  3. We contact the patient to schedule and confirm insurance.

After the visit

You will promptly receive a consultation note with the diagnosis, test results, and plan. Patients return to your care for ongoing ophthalmic or neurological management, with continued co-management where it helps.

Common reasons to refer

  • Optic disc swelling or pallor
  • Unexplained vision loss or visual field defect
  • New double vision, ptosis, or anisocoria
  • Suspected optic neuritis or demyelinating disease
  • Known or suspected IIH, adult or pediatric
  • Subjective visual disturbance or visual snow
  • Blepharospasm or hemifacial spasm for Botox
  • Visual complaints after stroke, head injury, or brain surgery
  • Pediatric optic nerve abnormalities, nystagmus, or strabismus with neurological features

Urgent concerns

Once the office is open, please call directly about suspected giant cell arteritis, new papilledema, or acute vision loss. Send any patient with signs of stroke or an acute neurological deficit to the nearest emergency department first.
