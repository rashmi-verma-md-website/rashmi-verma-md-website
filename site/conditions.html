Conditions we treat

Expert care for complex vision and neurological conditions

Many conditions that affect your vision have neurological causes, and they need a specialist trained in both fields to diagnose and treat them.

What is neuro-ophthalmology?

Neuro-ophthalmology focuses on vision problems related to the nervous system: the optic nerves, the visual pathways in the brain, and the nerves that control eye movement. If your eye doctor has ruled out a problem with the eye itself, or your vision symptoms come with headaches or other neurological symptoms, a neuro-ophthalmologist may be the right specialist.

Adult and pediatric care

Many neuro-ophthalmic conditions affect children differently than adults. Dr. Verma's dual fellowship training in pediatric ophthalmology and neuro-ophthalmology equips her to care for patients of all ages.

Conditions we diagnose and treat

Optic nerve

Optic neuritis

Inflammation of the optic nerve, often causing sudden vision loss, pain with eye movement, and faded colors. Frequently associated with multiple sclerosis.

Optic nerve

Papilledema and idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH)

Swelling of the optic disc from raised pressure inside the skull. IIH is a common cause, particularly in women of childbearing age, and can lead to permanent vision loss if untreated.

Optic nerve

Ischemic and other optic neuropathies

Damage to the optic nerve from reduced blood flow (AION, PION, including evaluation for giant cell arteritis), compression, hereditary conditions such as Leber's, or toxic and nutritional causes.

Eye movement

Double vision (diplopia)

Seeing two images of one object, caused by misalignment of the eyes from problems with the nerves or muscles that move them. It can be a sign of a serious neurological condition.

Eye movement

Cranial nerve palsies

Weakness of the 3rd, 4th, or 6th cranial nerve, causing double vision, a drooping eyelid, or abnormal eye position. Causes include diabetes, high blood pressure, and aneurysm.

Neuromuscular

Ocular myasthenia gravis

An autoimmune condition causing drooping eyelids and double vision that change through the day.

Visual pathway

Visual field loss

Loss of side or central vision from damage anywhere from the retina to the back of the brain. The pattern of loss helps locate the underlying problem.

Visual pathway

Vision changes after stroke

Stroke affecting the visual pathways can cause loss of half the visual field (hemianopia), visual neglect, or cortical visual impairment.

Eyelid and face

Blepharospasm and hemifacial spasm

Involuntary eyelid closing or twitching of one side of the face, treated with Botox injections in the office.

Other

Unexplained vision loss

Vision changes that a standard eye exam does not explain.

Other

Unequal or abnormal pupils

Including anisocoria and Horner syndrome.

Other

Migraine with visual symptoms

Visual aura and other visual disturbances that need evaluation to rule out other causes.

Not sure if your symptoms are neuro-ophthalmic?

Many patients come to Dr. Verma after seeing other specialists without a clear diagnosis. If you have unexplained vision changes, eye movement problems, or vision symptoms with neurological complaints, an evaluation may provide answers.
