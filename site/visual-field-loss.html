Conditions we treat

Visual Field Loss

Your visual field is everything you can see while looking straight ahead, including your side vision. Loss of part of the visual field can come from damage anywhere along the pathway from the eye to the back of the brain. The pattern of loss helps show where the problem is.

When to get emergency care

Sudden loss of vision in one or both eyes can be a sign of stroke. Call 911 right away.

Signs you may notice

  • Bumping into things or people on one side
  • Missing words or losing your place when reading
  • People or cars seeming to appear out of nowhere
  • Trouble driving or judging your surroundings

Many people do not notice the loss at first, because the brain fills in the gaps.

Common patterns and causes

  • Loss in one eye: usually a problem with the retina or optic nerve, such as optic neuritis, glaucoma or reduced blood flow to the optic nerve
  • Loss of the outer side of vision in both eyes: often from pressure on the optic chiasm, where the optic nerves cross, most commonly by a pituitary tumor
  • Loss of the same side of vision in both eyes (hemianopia): from damage behind the optic chiasm, such as stroke, tumor or head injury

How it is diagnosed

  • Automated visual field testing to map exactly which areas are affected
  • OCT imaging of the optic nerve and retina
  • A complete neuro-ophthalmic exam
  • MRI or other imaging when needed to find the cause

Treatment and rehabilitation

Treatment focuses on the cause. For example, vision may improve after treatment of a pituitary tumor, and some loss after stroke improves in the first months.

When loss is permanent, rehabilitation can help you make the most of the vision you have: scanning techniques, reading strategies, prism glasses and referral to low vision and occupational therapy services. Dr. Verma can also advise you about driving safety.

How Dr. Verma can help

Dr. Verma is trained in both neurology and neuro-ophthalmology. She looks at the eye, the optic nerve and the brain together, explains your results in plain language, and works closely with your eye doctor, neurologist and other specialists.
