Conditions we treat

Double Vision (Diplopia)

Double vision, or diplopia, means seeing two images of one object. The images may be side by side, one above the other, or tilted. Finding the cause is important, because double vision can be the first sign of a nerve, muscle or brain problem.

When to get emergency care

Call 911 if double vision starts suddenly together with a droopy eyelid, a severe headache, weakness, numbness, slurred speech or trouble swallowing.

One eye or both eyes?

Cover one eye, then the other.

  • If the double vision goes away when either eye is covered, the eyes are not lined up. This is called binocular double vision, and it is the type a neuro-ophthalmologist evaluates.
  • If it stays when you look with only one eye, the cause is usually in the eye itself, such as dry eye, cataract or a glasses prescription. Your regular eye doctor can help with this.

What causes it?

  • Cranial nerve palsies: weakness of the nerves that move the eyes, from diabetes, high blood pressure, aneurysm, trauma, tumor or stroke
  • Myasthenia gravis: an autoimmune condition that causes double vision and droopy eyelids that often worsen as the day goes on
  • Thyroid eye disease: swelling of the eye muscles
  • Childhood eye misalignment (strabismus) that becomes harder to control over time
  • Conditions affecting the brainstem, such as stroke or multiple sclerosis

How it is diagnosed

Dr. Verma measures how your eyes line up in different directions using prisms, examines eye movements, eyelids and pupils, and checks for fatigue. Depending on the findings, blood tests or brain imaging may be needed.

Treatment

  • Treating the cause, such as myasthenia gravis or thyroid eye disease
  • Watchful waiting: many nerve palsies from diabetes or high blood pressure improve on their own within about three months
  • Relief while you recover: patching one eye or temporary stick-on prism lenses
  • Prism glasses for long-term double vision
  • Referral for eye muscle surgery when double vision is stable and does not resolve

How Dr. Verma can help

Dr. Verma is trained in both neurology and neuro-ophthalmology. She looks at the eye, the optic nerve and the brain together, explains your results in plain language, and works closely with your eye doctor, neurologist and other specialists.
