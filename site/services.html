Services

Comprehensive neuro-ophthalmic evaluation and care

Every visit begins with a thorough evaluation and testing that combines the precision of ophthalmology with the diagnostic depth of neurology.

Comprehensive neuro-ophthalmic evaluation

A detailed exam of the visual system and its neurological connections: visual acuity, color vision, stereo acuity, pupils, eye movements, and a dilated exam to evaluate the optic nerve. Dr. Verma combines the eye exam with a neurological assessment to find where the problem lies, whether in the eye, optic nerve, visual pathway, or brain.

Who it is for: Unexplained vision loss, visual field defects, double vision, optic nerve abnormalities, or vision symptoms with neurological complaints.

OCT imaging of the optic nerve and retina

Optical coherence tomography (OCT) is a quick, painless scan that measures the optic nerve and retinal nerve fiber layer closely, detecting swelling, thinning, and changes invisible to the naked eye. It is especially useful for monitoring papilledema in IIH, recovery from optic neuritis, and changes to optic atrophy.

Who it is for: Papilledema, optic neuritis, IIH, optic neuropathy, or any condition needing optic nerve monitoring over time.

Visual field testing

Automated perimetry maps your side and central vision, detects blind spots, and tracks change over time. Results are interpreted alongside the full clinical picture.

Who it is for: Suspected or known optic nerve disease, raised intracranial pressure, stroke, or any condition affecting the visual pathway.

Botox injections for blepharospasm and hemifacial spasm

In-office onabotulinumtoxinA (Botox) injections to relax the muscles that cause involuntary eyelid closing or facial spasm. Treatment is usually repeated about every three months.

Who it is for: Adults with benign essential blepharospasm or hemifacial spasm.

Pediatric neuro-ophthalmic evaluation

Children can't always describe their symptoms, and their visual systems are still developing. Dr. Verma performs thorough, child-friendly evaluations and explains findings clearly to families.

Who it is for: Children referred for optic nerve swelling, unexplained vision loss, nystagmus, abnormal eye movements, or suspected intracranial hypertension.

IIH diagnosis and management

Ongoing care for idiopathic intracranial hypertension, including papilledema grading, visual field and OCT monitoring at each visit, and coordination of medical and surgical treatment with neurology and neurosurgery.

Who it is for: Adults and children with known or suspected IIH or raised intracranial pressure.

Co-management with neurology and neurosurgery

Pituitary tumors, multiple sclerosis, stroke, and intracranial hypertension often need care across specialties. Dr. Verma works closely with referring physicians and colleagues.

Who it is for: Patients with pituitary or sellar tumors, MS-related vision symptoms, stroke, or conditions needing several specialists.

Telehealth follow-up visits

Secure video visits for appropriate follow-ups, such as reviewing test results. Our office will let you know if your visit qualifies.

Who it is for: Established patients whose follow-up does not need an in-person exam.

Your first visit

  1. Referral and scheduling

    Most patients are referred by their ophthalmologist, neurologist, or primary care physician. Self-referrals are welcome.

  2. Comprehensive evaluation

    A detailed history, a full neuro-ophthalmic exam, and any testing needed, such as visual fields or OCT, in a single appointment when possible.

  3. Clear explanation

    Dr. Verma walks you through her findings, explains the diagnosis in plain language, and discusses your options.

  4. Coordinated follow-up

    If you need monitoring or co-management, Dr. Verma coordinates directly with your care team.
